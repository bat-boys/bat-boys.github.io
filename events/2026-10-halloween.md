# The Homestead Event of 2026

Event area location `Grassy Fields [368,539] in southern Laenor`

Quickstart:
- First, go get `orders` from the event area. This autoloads on entry.
- Then get some mob to near death and `conscript <mob>`. The bigger the better. You can
  check if the mob is suitable before killing. And there's a cooldown if conscripting
  fails.
- Back at area, `contribute orders to village` to move that mob to your village.
- Then you can check `village daily` and `village weekly` for tasks.
- On weekend boots (fri-sat-sun), you should do `village defend`.
- On monday boot, gathered supplies are moved to stockpile.
- Event ends on Sunday 11th of November

## Batrooms

Closets `White wooden door (closed) leads closet.` spawn inside areas and they
contain `storage crate with red painted stripes`. Door can be opened with lock biter.
You can `ransack crate` for comfort items and supply bags. It's customary to bring the 
crates back to event area so that all other players can ransack them as well.

Inside closet, `move shelf`, `step rectangle`. The area inside closets is *really* big, 
rooms are from random locations and don't give explore. There are three levels going down:
1. Summonable, contains crates that can be transported outside and small mobs, most
   don't see invisible
2. Here you can't talk with tells, channels or emotes. Bigger mobs, better crates.
3. Can't speak either. Even bigger mobs and crates that have to be ransacked in the room.
   The one-off mob `massive five-headed dragon stuck in a hole / Tii-A'mut` was also here.

There are really few exists down and lots of exits up, so getting out is fairly easy.

## `village help`

The main point of this event is to build, maintain, and upgrade your village. This is done
through several steps.

### Gather Villagers

Villagers are picked up through the use of conscription orders. While in combat with a
monster of a playable race that's badly injured, you can try to force it to surrender,
then bring it back here to put it to work for you. Tougher monsters make higher tier
villagers with more and higher-level skills.

For obvious gameplay/safety reasons, a lot of monsters resist being captured. This
includes most guild monsters, monsters that block exits, and very high level monsters.
Immune monsters won't consume your orders.

The maximum number of villagers you can have is six. You cannot house more villagers than
it can feed, but most villagers have at least one food skill.

### Put Them To Work

Villages don't need to be micromanaged. They'll use their skills to build up 'supplies'
using their skills. The higher their skills, the more supplies. Each of the skills is
weighted the same for the purposes of supplies.

Farming, Foraging, Hunting, and Fishing are food skills. Mining, Lumberjacking, Skinning
and Trading are repair skills.

### Raise the Comfort Level

While your villagers have basic buildings and land to gather resources, they don't start
with anything else. Tables and chairs, plates and silverware, doors and light sources -
all sorts of things can turn your village into a place to live, not just a few empty
rooms.

Comfort items are classified by their quality. Random items are typically not very
effective, but better than nothing. Be on the lookout for better items, or maybe try to
have them made.

A comfortable villager is a productive villager. The higher your village's comfort, the
more 'supplies' your villagers will provide. Getting some comfort level quickly will
therefore have the greatest overall effect in the Event.

Some comfort items, like drinking vessels and chairs, are needed in a full dozen. Others,
like fountains, flags, and musical instruments, are only needed singly. Your village's
comfort level is formed by the best five items in each of those groups, so you don't need
to fill out every single option.

### Perform Daily and Weekly Quests

Every day, your village will ask for help in five ways. Doing the first will give your
village 50 'stockpile' and the others 25 each.

Every Monday, your village will ask for help in three larger ways. Doing the first will
give your village 750 'stockpile' and the others 100 each.

Most of these quests will require you to hunt down creatures, find and bring back items,
or get eq. Most of these quests are easier with help.

### Defend Your Village From Raids

Every weekend, you village will be attacked. You can either defend it yourself, or arm
them and hope they defend themselves.

If you choose to defend your village personally, you'll chase down the raiders nearby and
fight them. Expect a tough fight, which will be stronger depending on the tier of your
village - villages with more supplies are more lucrative targets and attract higher-level
raiders. Killing them off will spare your village the raid entirely.

If you want the village to defend itself, they'll need armour and weapons. The better
weapons and armour they have, the better they'll do in the raid. Depending on their ac
(armour class) and wc (weapon class), the villagers could either suffer a total loss or a
complete victory, keeping anywhere from 0% to 100% of their 'supplies'. Any 'supplies'
they protect become 'stockpile'. How quickly the village recovers after damage depends on
its total repair skill.

Any 'stockpile' defended from raids, or earned by quests, are immune to raids. These are
the event points you'll spend on prizes at the end of the Event. The maximum 'stockpile'
you can possibly get is about 3,000 per week.
